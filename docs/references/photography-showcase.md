# Photography showcase source pack

Source and asset reference for the photography lesson. Claims are scoped to the institutional records below, rather than a complete global history. Source access checked 2026-11-23.

## Core institutional sources

| Source | Supported claims and limits |
| --- | --- |
| [Harry Ransom Center: The Niépce Heliograph](https://www.hrc.utexas.edu/niepce-heliograph/) | The custodian identifies the surviving camera-obscura image as 1827. Niépce's earlier camera pictures were fleeting; his heliograph used bitumen on pewter and required several days of sunlight. Say **earliest surviving camera photograph**, rather than proving who first made any photograph. |
| [The Met: Daguerre and the invention of photography](https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/daguerre-1787-1851-and-the-invention-of-photography) | Camera obscura projected an image before photography could preserve it. Niépce and Daguerre partnered in 1829. Daguerre's results were shown in January 1839; the process was publicly explained on 19 August. A daguerreotype is a unique image on silver-plated copper. Light, exposure, development and fixing are distinct stages. |
| [The Met: Talbot and the invention of photography](https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/william-henry-fox-talbot-1800-1877-and-the-invention-of-photography) | Talbot experimented with salt and silver nitrate on paper in 1834, made camera pictures in 1835, and disclosed his process in early 1839. His developed calotype was patented in February 1841. Herschel's proposed hypo fixer improved permanence; early experiments and later developed negatives must not be treated as one unchanged technique. |
| [V&A: An A to Z of photographic processes](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/photographic-processes) | A calotype paper negative permits multiple positive prints. Paper fibres could affect print detail. This supports a comparison of material, reproduction and texture, rather than a claim that one process was simply superior. |
| [The Met: The Pencil of Nature](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/267022) | Talbot and Henneman pursued editions of prints through a Reading establishment. The book appeared in parts in 1844–46 with 24 plates and proposed uses including documents, architecture, inventory, botanical records and art. Commercial success was not automatic: the project was discontinued as sales fell. |
| [Getty: The 19th-Century Selfie Pioneer](https://www.getty.edu/news/hippolyte-bayard-19th-century-selfie-pioneer/) | Bayard constructed self-portraits and staged *The Drowned Man* in 1840 with changing props and poses. His accompanying text complained about recognition. This is a concrete early example of a photograph depicting a staged situation; do not present it as a documentary death scene. |

## Three-chapter teaching sequence

### 1. Light could draw; keeping the drawing was harder

Begin with the distinction between an optical projection and a lasting object. The camera obscura did not itself solve how to preserve its image. Niépce's work joins optics to light-sensitive chemistry; material and time are part of the invention, rather than incidental obstacles. Use **ca. 1826–27** as a qualified overview date if needed, with the disclosure that the current Ransom Center record dates its surviving plate to **1827** (The Met's overview describes results by 1826). Avoid a universal eight-hour exposure claim: the current custodian describes several days. These dates concern a surviving object and evolving experiments, not a single uncontested birthday for all photography. Sources: Ransom Center and Met Daguerre essay above.

### 2. One plate, or an image that could travel through copies?

Compare two material paths: a daguerreotype's unique metal plate, and a paper negative used to produce positive prints. Distinguish the 1835 Talbot experiment shown here from his 1841 patented calotype. Explain tonal reversal through the actual Oriel Window object: the brighter window darkened the sensitive paper more. Explain repeatability with a process diagram, rather than pretending the included negative and *The Open Door* form a matching pair. Paper's texture could soften printed detail; the capacity to make copies changed what the image could do. Sources: Met Talbot essay and V&A process glossary above; the Oriel Window record below.

### 3. A picture can circulate and still need interpretation

Move from an individual object to print production and the illustrated book. *The Pencil of Nature* tested practical uses and a commercial market; its end cautions against a frictionless story of inevitable adoption. Then ask what a photograph actually establishes. *The Open Door* was a conscious composition informed by Dutch genre painting, while Bayard explicitly staged a fictive death. **Interpretation:** chemical contact with light does not remove choices of scene, position, pose, exposure and accompanying words. Avoid inferring the precise exposure or an empty city from the lack of people in the Trafalgar Square image. Sources: Met book record, Getty Bayard article, and Open Door record below.
