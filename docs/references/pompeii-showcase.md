# Pompeii showcase source pack

Source and asset reference for Pompeii. Claims are scoped to the archaeological authority, a university edition of an ancient source, and institutions discussing their own collections/research. Source access checked 2026-11-23.

## Chapter 1 — A city that worked

**Argument:** Read the city through the work needed to keep it running. A mill, an oven and a washing basin connect buildings to food, textiles and labor. They do not give us a complete biography of an individual worker.

### S1. Archaeological Park of Pompeii — Bakery of Popidio Prisco

[Institutional site record](https://pompeiisites.org/en/archaeological-site/bakery-of-popidio-prisco/)

- Five lava millstones survive here. Their upper, hourglass-shaped stones turned around conical lower stones to grind grain.
- The Park describes the mills as powered by enslaved people or animals. A large oven occupied the building.
- A sales counter is absent in this bakery. Made-to-order production, wholesale sale or distribution by street vendors are interpretations offered by the Park, not a surviving transaction record.
- **Safe use:** a grain → flour → bread sequence; a material-evidence hotspot on a millstone. Do not invent a named baker's schedule or claim every bakery used an identical sales system.

### S2. Archaeological Park of Pompeii — Fullery of Stephanus

[Institutional site record](https://pompeiisites.org/en/archaeological-site/fullery-of-stephanus/)

- A house was converted into a facility for cleaning laundry and treating newly woven cloth late in the city's life.
- An atrium basin replaced the rainwater basin; further tubs were installed toward the rear. The upper area served for drying laundry.
- The Park describes workers, predominantly enslaved, treading cloth for hours in a treatment liquid containing urine.
- Electoral inscriptions underlie the association with Stephanus. The proposed identification of a skeleton near the entrance as the owner attempting to escape with takings is explicitly supposition. Omit that individualized story from declarative lesson copy.
- **Safe use:** contrast the attractive domestic courtyard with repetitive labor. State enslavement directly; do not present all inhabitants as comfortably wealthy citizens.

## Chapter 2 — An eruption, and the evidence it left

**Argument:** The disaster unfolded through different hazards. Written testimony and deposits preserve different kinds of information; later casts add another layer of mediation.

### S3. Archaeological Park of Pompeii — The Casts

[Institutional explanation](https://pompeiisites.org/en/pompeii-map/analysis/the-casts/)

- The Park distinguishes an earlier fall of pumice/lapilli, including deaths associated with collapsing structures, from the later arrival of a hot pyroclastic flow.
- Fine ash retained outlines after buried bodies decomposed. Casts were produced using the method developed by Giuseppe Fiorelli from 1863 onward.
- The page also documents damage to casts during bombing in 1943 and subsequent restoration, as well as modern scanning and copies.
- **Safe use:** “A plaster cast records a void left in volcanic deposits.” Avoid calling a cast a petrified body or an untouched ancient sculpture. Avoid a blanket claim that everyone died simultaneously, precise temperatures, or one universal cause of death; this introductory Park page simplifies a complex research question.

### S4. Pliny the Younger, *Letters* 6.20 — Dickinson College Commentaries

[University text and commentary](https://dcc.dickinson.edu/pliny-letters/6-20)

- Pliny recounts his own experience with his mother at Misenum: worsening tremors, fear of structural collapse, departure, darkness and ash. These are events in his account, not observations from Pompeii's streets.
- The commentary dates the letter probably to late AD 106 or early 107, decades after the eruption. The addressee is Tacitus.
- The text includes both reported rumors and Pliny's assessment that some were false. Its literary construction and retrospective standpoint matter.
- **Safe use:** distinguish “Pliny reports…” from a modern reconstruction. A map can separate Misenum from Pompeii, making the limits of his viewpoint visible. Do not transplant his personal actions to a fictional Pompeian resident or treat every statement as direct observation.

## Chapter 3 — A city still being interpreted

**Argument:** Excavation makes evidence accessible and exposes it to new damage. Scientific work can overturn stories attached to finds, while leaving names, relationships and experiences unknown.

### S5. Getty Research Institute — Pompeian Interiors Exposed

[Getty's account of its exhibition and records](https://www.getty.edu/news/new-exhibition-offered-look-inside-pompeiis-interiors/)

- Getty compares early prints and photographs with later views to show losses and conservation interventions after excavation.
- At the House of the Tragic Poet, its illustrated comparison identifies faded color, missing stucco and modern roofing/mesh used to protect surviving surfaces.
- The article describes decorated houses and villas but does not make them representative of every resident's housing.
- **Safe use:** an early record beside a present-day view can ask what exposure changes. Label the dates and media. Historical pictures can document appearance and interpretation; they are not automatically accurate reconstructions of AD 79.
- The article's modern Hartill photograph is copyrighted; it was **not** downloaded or licensed for this pack.

### S6. Max Planck Society — DNA evidence and Pompeii casts

[Researchers' institutional report, 7 November 2024](https://www.mpg.de/23699890/dna-evidence-rewrites-pompeii-eruption-story)

- Researchers extracted DNA from fragmented skeletal remains within casts undergoing restoration.
- An adult wearing a gold bracelet and associated with a child, long described as a mother with her child, was genetically male and not biologically related to that child.
- A pair interpreted as sisters or a mother and daughter included at least one genetically male individual.
- **Safe use:** visual proximity, jewelry and a compelling pose cannot by themselves establish biological kinship or gender. DNA does not reveal friendship, care, affection, a person's name or social gender identity. Do not replace an unsupported family story with an equally unsupported story about lovers or strangers.
- The sampled individuals cannot stand in for all Pompeians. Keep any ancestry claim confined to the studied sample; the lesson does not need a population-wide percentage.

## Editorial limits for all chapters

- Use **AD 79** without a specific month/day. This pack does not adjudicate the disputed eruption date.
- Avoid “the whole city froze in one instant.” Keep the sequence of hazards and the later history of excavation, display and conservation visible.
- A ruined street photographed today, an 1804 etching, an 1819 painting and an ancient wall surface are different evidence types. Put that distinction in the visible caption.
- Do not invent dialogue, residents' private thoughts, named victims or a complete evacuation/death toll from this evidence.
- Enslaved workers and people without surviving personal narratives should be acknowledged without pretending the sources supply their individual voices.
- No direct source quotations are used in this pack; all narrative notes are paraphrases.
